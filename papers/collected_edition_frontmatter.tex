\clearpage\section{Series Summary}\label{series-summary}
{\sffamily\color{teal}\large From correct answers to traceable records and decisions\par}\medskip
A correct answer can carry an incorrect source identifier. A system can faithfully read a stale record or faithfully follow a poor agreement. These four exploratory reports examine what must be evaluated separately when LLM roles retain their own information and judgments while sharing records and coordinating action. Synthetic two- and three-role tasks trace generated responses through actual saved notes or votes, the inputs supplied to later calls, and subsequent use.

\textbf{Paper 1 — Content and provenance.} Correct decisions coexisted with incorrect origin IDs that persisted into later responses. Model-based selection followed by deterministic copying preserved the targeted shared observations, while defects in other fields and subsequent readout prevented an established end-to-end advantage over rewriting. A revocation diagnostic distinguished completing a justified change from appropriately deferring when its target could not be identified.

\textbf{Paper 2 — Scope of update control.} A correct joint answer could conceal stale private observations. Conversely, repairing those observations could leave overall success unchanged because a shared-field defect remained. Comparisons using the same update proposals exposed both relationships. Continued use supported two cycles of observation updates, yet a separate customer-update diagnostic showed old notices undoing legitimate new preferences.

\textbf{Paper 3 — Judgment and agreement.} Supplying computational summaries earlier supported correct intermediate role-specific judgments, even when final-use outcomes were correct under both timing conditions. Correct own judgments and available candidate totals nevertheless coexisted with acceptance of a suboptimal agreement, which was then used faithfully. Fixed-input repetitions further showed that the same combined failure could conceal different defective components.

\textbf{Paper 4 — Actual votes and approval states.} A recorded hold on an appropriate proposal was faithfully respected by subsequent readers. In a matched three-state comparison, one missing vote became an explicit hold in the readout while agreement and execution were correctly deferred. Appropriate voting, fidelity to received votes, and accurate individual approval records therefore require separate checks.

\textbf{Technical synthesis — From observations to design.} Six failure observations motivate seven design principles, a reference architecture, and executable examples. The central task is to preserve whose information or expressed position justifies each next action: bind content to provenance, identify the scope of updates and controls, separate own judgments from joint assignments, and distinguish proposal assessment, actual votes, agreement, and execution authorization.

\textbf{Scope.} The collection retains each study's evaluation units and limitations; counts are not pooled. Small synthetic tasks do not establish general reliability or the causal effect of increasing role count. The synthesis adds no new experiment, and the complete proposed architecture has not been validated in an end-to-end LLM comparison.
